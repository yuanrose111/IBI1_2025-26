This practical compared protein sequences using online BLAST and a custom Python non‑gapped alignment program with the BLOSUM62 matrix.
The human DLX5 protein (UniProt ID: P56178) is 289 amino acids long and localised to the nucleus. A BLAST search against Eutheria returned 250 hits, with identities ranging from 52.3% to 100%.

Three Python alignments were performed:

Human DLX5 vs. Mouse DLX5 (UniProt ID: P70396)
Percentage identity: 96.54% (consistent with the 96.5% reported in public databases)

Human DLX5 vs. Random sequence
Total BLOSUM62 score: -259
Percentage identity: 5.19%

Mouse DLX5 vs. Random sequence
Total BLOSUM62 score: -269
Percentage identity: 4.84%

The human–mouse pair yielded a very high percentage identity of 96.54%, confirming their close evolutionary relationship. 
In contrast, both comparisons involving the random sequence produced very low scores and identities near 5%, which is the expected background similarity for unrelated proteins. 
These results clearly demonstrate that human and mouse DLX5 are evolutionarily closely related homologous proteins, while random sequences show no meaningful similarity.
